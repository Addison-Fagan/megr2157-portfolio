Task B:  Product Analysis: Yeti Tumbler Lid https://patents.google.com/patent/USD751339S1/en

The item I chose to analyse, the slide lid of the Yeti tumbler, basically works by using a little sliding piece that opens and closes over the drink hole so you can choose when to take a sip and stop your drink from spilling or getting dirt in it. It presses tightly into the top of the cup so it stays stuck in place without leaking around the edges, and keeping the lid shut also traps the heat or cold inside so your drink doesn't go warm or melt too fast. The ingenious part of the lid is the fact that the lid is magnetic and can be easily removed for cleaning or replacement. The primary function of the slide lid is to regulate gravity, and pressure-driven liquid discharge through a variable opening. This is governed by the Orifice Flow Equation, which is partially derived from Bernoulli’s principle. My assumption is that the liquid (typically water or coffee) has a constant density and flows under negligible turbulence-induced compressibility effects as it exits the aperture.

https://patentimages.storage.googleapis.com/7e/5c/92/a6d9294fa1d54e/USD0751339-20160315-D00001.png

Based on the image above, the geometry of the slide lid directly dictates its mechanical performance by regulating fluid flow, guiding kinematic motion, ensuring vessel retention, and maintaining thermal and fluid containment. Its rounded rectangular dispensing aperture with filleted edges minimizes viscous flow separation, while the planar slider constrained to a single linear degree of freedom by the raised lateral walls of the recessed guide track acts as a sliding gate valve to continuously adjust the exposed flow area from fully closed to fully open. An upstream secondary vent port allows atmospheric air into the vessel headspace during liquid discharge to equalize pressure and prevent flow glugging, while the stepped outer cylindrical skirt and circumferential gasket channel provide a uniform radial interference fit against the tumbler’s inner rim to prevent perimeter leakage and resist hydrostatic pressure. Finally, the solid polymer cross-section and defined wall thickness eliminate hollow gaps, offering structural rigidity against insertion deflection while establishing a conductive resistance barrier that reduces heat transfer across the beverage boundary.

The patent number for the lid is US D751,339 S, invented by Roy Joseph Seiders and Steve Nichols for YETI Coolers, LLC. Two alternative solutions that accomplish the same primary flow-gating function are spring-loaded push-button valves like the Contigo Autoseal, which translate axially to seal automatically upon release, and hinged flip-top caps like the Hydro Flask flip lid, which uses rotational motion to seat a plug via an interference fit. An evident design decision visible in the patent geometry is the open, shallow recessed linear guide track rather than an enclosed, captured slider channel; the engineers likely chose this low-profile, open configuration to prevent beverage residue and bacteria from becoming trapped in hard-to-clean crevices, simplify injection-molding tooling by eliminating complex internal undercuts, and keep the slider nearly flush with the lid deck to avoid accidental snagging during transit.
